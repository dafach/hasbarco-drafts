How to apply — in three steps

We keep it straightforward. The Housing Authority of the County of Santa Barbara runs these homes — call and a real person will help you, in English or Spanish.

  1. 1

Do you qualify?

Creekside is income-restricted — rent is set below market for families within certain income limits. The limit depends on how many people live in your home. Many apartments are reserved for lower-income families, so don’t rule yourself out — ask us and we’ll check.

🚜 Farmworker preference

You or a member of your household may qualify for a farmworker preference on the waitlist if you currently work in agriculture. This can improve your placement and shorten your wait. To verify, upload one of:

  • A letter from your agricultural employer
  • Recent pay stubs showing agricultural work
  • Self-employment records related to farm labor

Frequently asked questions

Who can live at Creekside Village?
Families earning within the income limits for their household size. Homes are two, three, and four bedrooms, for households of about 2 to 8 people. If you or someone in your home works in agriculture, you may qualify for a farmworker preference on the waitlist.
Do I have to be a farmworker to apply?
No. Anyone who meets the income limits can apply. But if you or a household member currently works in agriculture, you can claim a farmworker preference that may move you up the waitlist and shorten your wait.
How much is rent?
Rent is based on your income and apartment size. Two-bedrooms run about $1,191–$2,383/month, three-bedrooms $1,377–$2,754, and four-bedrooms $1,536–$3,072 — with many homes set at the lower, deeper-affordable end. The owner pays electricity, water, sewer, trash, and internet; you pay only gas.
Do I need to prove U.S. citizenship?
No. Proof of U.S. citizenship is not required to apply for Creekside Village.
What do I need to apply?
Bring your ID documents and income verification. For the farmworker preference, bring one of: a letter from your agricultural employer, recent pay stubs showing farm work, or self-employment records related to farm labor. Ask us and we’ll walk you through it.
Can you help me in Spanish?
Yes — our team can help you in English or Spanish. If you speak Mixteco or another Indigenous language, we can arrange an interpreter service. Ask us through the form below and we’ll find someone who can help.
